WHY THE DOCUMENT MATTERS

When a discussion turns to “ethical hunting,” government records show that hunting often exists within a wider system: agency stocking for recreational harvest, publicly financed wildlife management, predator suppression to aid game recruitment, and lethal wildlife control to protect farmed animals. Those are distinct activities. This resource presents the agency’s own program description, its documented quantities or budgets where reported, and the primary source, so the evidence—not an unsupported generalization—can be discussed.

The presence of a statewide funding allocation does not prove that the full allocation was spent killing predators or stocking animals. Likewise, killing undertaken to protect livestock is not automatically killing undertaken to increase deer populations. The documented purpose of each action is stated separately.

WILDLIFE REMOVAL | FY2025: USDA LETHALLY REMOVED ABOUT 2.83 MILLION ANIMALS

USDA Wildlife Services reports encountering about 28.1 million animals; it dispersed 25.2 million without killing them and lethally removed approximately 2.83 million (10.1%). USDA classifies 2,457,669 of those lethal removals as invasive species. These nationwide totals span multiple purposes beyond agriculture.

SOURCE aphis.usda.gov — view original record

HOW TO READ THE NUMBERS AND CLAIMS

Hunting equipment excise taxes, fishing-related taxes, license fees, federal appropriations, grants and industry assessments are different funding sources. A government-supervised livestock checkoff is not automatically a taxpayer subsidy. A wildlife removal is not automatically a livestock-related killing.

WHAT THE RECORDS SHOW: Public agencies breed and release some animals for recreation, pay for or conduct predator removal intended to increase some hunted populations, carry out lethal control in certain livestock conflicts, and fund some meat-processing activities. Program objectives are not proof of ecological success; not every wildlife removal is for hunting or agriculture, and not every hunted animal was stocked.

Terms matter: “Allocated” is an appropriation or apportionment, not necessarily spent. “Planned” is a proposed objective, not a confirmed outcome. “Released / stocked” does not mean caught or killed. “Lethally removed” is a reported death; “authorization” does not mean the killing happened. Historical project archives document the program at that time and should not be presented as the current year’s annual kill totals.

A government source can describe an intention to increase game populations by removing predators without proving that the ecological goal succeeded. Different objectives coexist: livestock protection, game harvest, subsistence harvest, conservation, airports and public safety. Read the purpose given in each agency record.

GO VEGAN • EVIDENCE FOR PUBLIC CONVERSATIONS

The factual question is not simply whether an individual hunter kills an animal. The broader question is which animal lives, which animal dies, and which public programs and economic interests influence those decisions. Use the sources above to examine each example, and distinguish the documentary fact from your own moral conclusion.
